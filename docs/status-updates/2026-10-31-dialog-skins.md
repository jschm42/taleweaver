# One adventure, many looks: introducing TaleWeaver's UI themes

A story's atmosphere doesn't stop at the words. A detective's case file, a walk
across a frozen landscape and a transmission from deep space should each feel
different before you read the first line.

TaleWeaver's new game UI themes bring that mood into the interface itself:
narration, dialogue and story tabs take on a look that fits the adventure.
Here are four examples.

![The Ancient game UI theme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jschm42/taleweaver/main/docs/screenshots/themes/theme-ancient.jpg)

**Ancient.** Warm ochre on dark leather frames a tense exchange in a shadowy
hall — portraits on the left, story and dialogue cards down the middle,
passages and discoveries on the right.

![The Eldritch game UI theme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jschm42/taleweaver/main/docs/screenshots/themes/theme-eldritch.jpg)

**Eldritch.** Acid-green accents over a dim industrial corridor, a lamp that
never quite reaches the corners. It reads like something is listening.

![The Noir game UI theme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jschm42/taleweaver/main/docs/screenshots/themes/theme-noir.jpg)

**Noir.** Cream, black and a red that only shows up when it matters. Two
portraits in a black-and-white diner, typewritten case-file text.

![The Terminal game UI theme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jschm42/taleweaver/main/docs/screenshots/themes/theme-terminal.jpg)

**Terminal.** Cyan scan lines and monospace readouts over a wrecked science
fiction corridor, with a medical locker opened into item cards.

## Make it yours

These four are a sample. There are eight built-in skins in total — Classic,
Chronicle, Noir, Terminal, Grimoire, Eldritch, Toon, Ancient — and they don't
just recolour text: each one brings its own bubble shapes, tab styling, dice
faces, scene tint and NPC entrances.

Authors pick a skin and an optional accent colour in the editor, with a live
preview rendered by the real skin classes. Leave it on **Auto** and the skin is
matched to the adventure's narrative tone instead. Players aren't locked in
either: a skin chosen in the in-game settings outranks the author's choice
everywhere.

Auto matches on keywords, not on meaning, so its pick isn't always the one
you'd expect — a tone like "medieval fantasy" lands on Chronicle while plain
"medieval" becomes Ancient. Overriding it takes one click.

Which look would you give your next adventure — and what story would you put
in it?
